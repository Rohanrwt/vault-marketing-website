# Objection Handling — Quick Reference

> **How to use:** Keep this open during sales calls. Find the objection, read the response, use the proof point.

---

## Price Objections

### "How much does this cost?"

**Real concern:** They want to see if you're in their range before committing time.

**Response:**
> "It depends on the scope — we build custom packages based on what you actually need. But to give you a ballpark: most clients invest ₹25K–₹75K/month including ad spend. What I can tell you is we generated ₹10 Crore from under ₹50K in ad spend for our last Real Estate client. I'll put exact numbers in the proposal."

**Follow-up:** "What's your current monthly marketing budget? That'll help me right-size the proposal."

---

### "That's too expensive" / "Other agencies are cheaper"

**Real concern:** They don't see the ROI yet, or they're comparing you to low-cost social media managers.

**Response:**
> "I get it — ₹50K/month sounds like a lot until you see the return. Our Dehradun client spent ₹50K on ads and closed ₹10 Crore in sales. The question isn't what you spend — it's what you get back. The 'cheaper' agency — what results did they deliver in numbers?"

**Follow-up:** "What did your last agency actually generate in leads or sales? Not impressions — actual leads."

---

### "No budget right now"

**Real concern:** Either genuinely can't afford it, or haven't prioritized it.

**Response:**
> "Understood. Can I ask — do you have units/rooms to sell right now? Because if inventory is sitting, the cost of NOT marketing is much higher than the cost of running ads. Every empty month is lost revenue that doesn't come back."

**Follow-up:** "What if we started small — say a ₹15K test budget for 2 weeks — and I showed you exactly what kind of leads that generates? No long-term commitment."

---

## Timing Objections

### "Not the right time" / "Maybe next quarter"

**Real concern:** They don't feel urgency, or they're avoiding a decision.

**Response:**
> "I hear that a lot. But here's the thing — your competitors are running ads right now, which means the leads that would've come to you are going to them. Every week you wait, your cost per lead goes up because the competition gets heavier. When's your next launch/season? If we start now, we'll have winning ads ready by then instead of scrambling."

**Follow-up:** "What would need to happen for this to become the right time?"

---

### "We're too busy to deal with this right now"

**Real concern:** They think working with an agency takes a lot of their time.

**Response:**
> "That's actually exactly why an agency makes sense. You don't need to do anything — we handle creative, ads, landing pages, follow-ups, everything. The only thing I need from you is a 30-minute kickoff call and approval on creatives. After that, we run it and you get the leads."

**Follow-up:** "If I could have your first ads live within 7 days with minimal effort from your side, would that work?"

---

## Competition Objections

### "We already have an agency" / "We already do this in-house"

**Real concern:** Switching costs, loyalty, or fear of change.

**Response:**
> "Great — that means you already know marketing matters. Quick question: what's your current cost per lead? And how many leads are you getting per month? I ask because a lot of clients come to us paying ₹800–₹2,000 per lead with their current agency. We typically get that down to under ₹200. Happy to do a free comparison — no commitment."

**Follow-up:** "Would it be useful if I ran a free audit of your current ads? I can show you exactly where leads are leaking without you changing anything."

---

### "What makes you different from other agencies?"

**Real concern:** They've been burned before and want proof.

**Response:**
> "Three things. One — we specialize in [Real Estate / Hospitality], so we know your buyer and your market. We're not a generic agency doing restaurants and salons and tech startups. Two — we've got verified results: ₹10 Crore in sales, 1000+ bookings, under ₹200 cost per lead. Three — you'll see exactly where every rupee goes. We share live dashboards, not monthly PDFs with vanity metrics."

**Follow-up:** "What went wrong with the last agency you worked with? I want to make sure we don't repeat that."

---

## Status Quo Objections

### "What we have works fine" / "We get enough business from referrals"

**Real concern:** No perceived need to change.

**Response:**
> "Referrals are great — and they should keep coming. But here's the risk: referrals are unpredictable. You can't control when they come or how many. What ads give you is a tap you can turn on — 10 leads this week, 30 next week, whatever you need. Think of it as adding a second engine, not replacing the first."

**Follow-up:** "If you could get 50 extra qualified leads next month on top of your referrals, what would that do for your business?"

---

### "We tried Facebook ads, didn't work"

**Real concern:** Past bad experience with a different agency or DIY attempt.

**Response:**
> "That's literally the most common thing I hear. And 9 out of 10 times, the issue is one of three things: bad targeting, weak creative, or no follow-up system. Not the channel itself. Facebook and Instagram are where your buyers spend 2+ hours a day — the platform works, the execution matters. Can I ask what the agency was doing specifically? I'll tell you honestly if the problem was strategy or if your market genuinely doesn't work on Meta."

**Follow-up:** "Would you be open to a 2-week test? If it doesn't generate leads at under ₹300 each, you walk away."

---

## Authority Objections

### "I need to discuss with my partner / boss"

**Real concern:** They're not the sole decision-maker.

**Response:**
> "Of course. Would it help if I put together a short one-pager with the key points that you can share with them? I can also jump on a quick call with both of you if that would make it easier."

**Follow-up:** "Who else would need to be involved? Happy to include them in our next conversation so everyone hears it firsthand."

---

### "Send me a proposal and I'll get back to you"

**Real concern:** Could be genuine, or could be a polite way to end the conversation.

**Response:**
> "Absolutely, I'll have that to you by [specific day]. Just so I can make it relevant — is there anything specific you'd want to see in the proposal that would help you make a decision? And when would be a good time for me to walk you through it? It's much clearer in a 10-minute call than reading it cold."

**Follow-up:** Always book a specific time. "I'll send it Thursday and walk you through it Friday at 11am — does that work?"

---

## Trust Objections

### "How do I know this will work for my project specifically?"

**Real concern:** They believe their market/project is different.

**Response:**
> "Fair question. Here's what I'd say — we've done this for plot sales in Dehradun, resort bookings across India, and car detailing in Canada. Three very different markets. The principles are the same: find the right audience, show them the right message, follow up fast. But we always start with a small test — ₹15K–₹25K — so you can see real data from YOUR market before committing to anything bigger."

**Follow-up:** "What would make you feel confident this would work? A specific lead volume? A cost per lead target?"

---

### "Can you guarantee results?"

**Real concern:** They want risk reversal.

**Response:**
> "I can't guarantee a specific number of sales — nobody honest can, because sales depend on your product, pricing, and sales team too. But I can guarantee three things: you'll get leads within the first 7 days, you'll see exactly where every rupee goes, and if the campaign isn't generating leads at a reasonable cost within 30 days, we'll pause and reassess together. No lock-in contracts."

**Follow-up:** "What result would make this a clear win for you? Let's set that as our target."
