Meta Ads for auto detailers in Canada: what actually works

We've run these campaigns for auto detailers in Calgary, Toronto, and Gurugram. Here's the creative format, targeting logic, and bidding approach that produces leads — not just impressions.

R
Rohan
Vault Marketing

Auto detailing is one of the most Meta-friendly service businesses there is. The results are visual. The before/after transformation is dramatic. The target customer is identifiable. The purchase decision is emotional.

And yet most auto detailing Meta campaigns are terrible. Generic carousel ads, stock photos of cars, "book now" CTAs pointing to a homepage, and a $10/day budget running on a broad "car enthusiasts" interest audience. Then the owner concludes Meta Ads don't work for their business.

They do. You're just running them wrong. Here's what we've learned running these campaigns.

The campaigns we've run

We've managed Meta Ads for auto detailers in Calgary, Alberta and Gurugram, India. Different markets, different budgets, different benchmarks — but the same core principles applied.

In Calgary, we took over a campaign that was already running. The owner was getting leads but the cost-per-lead was high enough that the economics didn't work at their service pricing. We rebuilt the campaign from scratch. Cost-per-lead dropped 55% within 90 days.

In Gurugram, we launched from zero for a new business entering a competitive local market. Their target was under ₹200/lead. We hit consistent under ₹100/lead within the first month.

Different benchmarks, same approach. Here's what it looked like.

Creative: what works and what doesn't

Before/after is not optional

The single highest-performing creative format for auto detailing, by a significant margin, is before/after. Not a polished marketing photo of a shiny car — an actual side-by-side showing the transformation from dirty/oxidized/scratched to clean/corrected/coated.

Why does it work? Because it proves the service without requiring the customer to trust your claims. You're not saying "we do amazing paint corrections." You're showing exactly what a paint correction does to a specific type of paint damage on a specific type of car. The customer self-identifies: "my car looks like that before, I want it to look like that after."

The format that works best is a vertical video (Reels/Stories placement) — not a static image. Film the before state, narrate what you're about to fix, show the process briefly, then reveal the after. 30–45 seconds is the sweet spot.

Service-specific beats generic

A campaign for "auto detailing" underperforms against a campaign for "ceramic coating" or "paint correction" or "interior deep clean." More specific creative attracts customers who want exactly that service — they're further down the decision funnel and more likely to book.

Run separate ad sets for each core service rather than one generic campaign. You'll pay more per click initially, but cost-per-booked-job will be lower because you're reaching higher-intent prospects.

Show the car, not the shop

Detailers love showing their facility. Customers don't care about your facility — they care about what happens to their car. Show the car at every stage. Hands working. Specific problem areas. The reaction after the reveal. Keep the environment as a backdrop, not the focus.

Targeting: radius, not interests

The mistake most detailers make with targeting is starting with interest audiences: "car enthusiasts," "luxury vehicles," "auto maintenance." These are too broad and not local enough.

Start with geographic radius targeting. 10–15km around your location (or the customer's location if you're mobile). People who need their car detailed want someone close. A customer in Airdrie is not going to drive to Calgary for a basic detail, no matter how good your ads are.

Layer demographic targeting only where it genuinely applies to your service mix. Ceramic coating skews toward car enthusiasts with newer/higher-value vehicles. Interior cleans after kids — parents with young families. Read your own customer data and apply it selectively, not as a default.

Lookalike audiences after you have data

Once you have 50–100 conversions (leads or purchases), build a lookalike audience from your customer list or from people who completed your lead form. This is when Meta Ads get significantly more efficient — the algorithm knows what your actual customers look like and finds more of them.

Don't try to build lookalikes before you have conversion data. The model won't have enough signal to be useful.

Offers: the friction-reducer

The biggest CPC-to-CPL gap in auto detailing campaigns is usually at the landing page. Clicks are cheap; bookings are expensive, because there's no compelling reason to book right now instead of "later."

An introductory offer closes that gap. Not a permanent discount — a limited-time or new-customer-specific offer. "First detail 15% off for new customers this month" changes the calculus from "I'll book sometime" to "I should book now."

Important: The offer needs to be on your landing page, not just in the ad. If someone clicks an ad promoting a new-customer discount and lands on a page with no mention of that discount, you'll lose most of them. Ad and landing page must match.

Landing page: one job

Your landing page has one job: get the lead. Not showcase your full service menu, not tell your story, not build trust with five paragraphs of copy. Get. The. Lead.

What that looks like in practice:

  • Service-specific headline that matches the ad ("Calgary paint correction from $XXX")
  • 3–5 before/after photos above the fold
  • A lead form or booking button within the first scroll
  • One clear CTA, repeated at the bottom
  • Trust signals: Google reviews count, specific results, real names if you have them

A homepage is not a landing page. Don't point ads at your homepage unless your homepage is actually built to convert paid traffic.

Budget and bidding

For a local market like Calgary or a Tier-2 Indian city, a realistic starting budget is CAD $30–50/day (or the local equivalent) for a single service campaign. Less than this and you won't generate enough impressions to optimize within a reasonable time frame. More than this before you have a converting campaign and you're just scaling losses.

Use Advantage+ placements and let Meta optimize placement across Facebook, Instagram, Reels, and Stories. Don't manually restrict placements unless you have clear data showing certain placements underperform.

For bidding, start with Lowest Cost (automatic bidding) while you're learning. Once you have cost-per-lead data and a target CPL, you can switch to Cost Cap to enforce a ceiling. But don't set a cost cap too early — it'll restrict delivery before the algorithm has enough data.

What the numbers looked like

In Calgary, before our rebuild: cost-per-lead was approximately CAD $85. After: consistently under CAD $40 within 90 days, settling around CAD $35–38 at steady state.

The changes that drove that improvement: creative (moved from static images to before/after video), radius-first targeting (removed the interest audiences that were pulling in non-local clicks), and a dedicated landing page per service (stopped pointing traffic to the homepage).

In Gurugram: ₹100/lead average across the campaign lifecycle, peaking as low as ₹72 in optimized weeks. The offer (introductory pricing on full interior detail) and before/after creative were the primary drivers.

The honest take

Meta Ads work for auto detailers when you treat the platform seriously — real creative, real landing pages, real offer, real targeting logic. The businesses that tell you "Meta doesn't work" are usually the ones running boosted posts of their Instagram photos with no specific offer and pointing traffic to their homepage.

The mechanics aren't complicated. Before/after creative. Specific service campaigns. Geographic radius targeting. A real offer. A landing page that converts. Enough budget to learn. That's it.
