How to rank on Gemini AI for local business searches

AI search is reshaping how local customers find businesses. Here's what Gemini AI actually looks for — and the practical steps to appear in AI-generated local results.

R
Rohan
Vault Marketing

Every few years, something genuinely changes in local search. Not "changes" like a minor algorithm update that your SEO agency will hype into a newsletter — but actually changes in how customers find local businesses. Gemini AI, and Google's broader push toward AI-generated answers, is one of those shifts.

The question most local business owners are asking is simple: if customers start getting answers from AI instead of clicking on search results, where does that leave me?

The answer is more nuanced than either "SEO is dead" or "nothing changes." Let's go through what's actually happening and what you can do about it.

What Gemini AI is doing to local search

Google's AI Overviews (powered by Gemini) now appear at the top of many search results — including local ones. When someone searches "best auto detailer in Calgary" or "plumber near me open now," they may see an AI-generated summary before any organic results or Map Pack listings.

That summary pulls from multiple sources: your Google Business Profile, your website content, third-party review sites, and other indexed pages about your business. Gemini is synthesizing signals, not just ranking pages.

What this means practically: it's not enough to rank on page one anymore. You need to be the kind of business that an AI feels confident recommending — which requires a different kind of signal than traditional SEO.

The signals Gemini uses to surface local businesses

Based on what we've observed across client accounts and publicly available research, here's what appears to matter most:

1. Google Business Profile completeness and recency

Your GBP is the most direct data source Gemini has about your business. Businesses with complete, recently updated profiles — full hours, current photos, active Q&A, regular posts — appear more frequently in AI-generated answers than businesses with thin or stale profiles.

This isn't just about having a profile. It's about the profile behaving like a live, active business. A GBP with 20 photos from 2021 and no recent activity sends a signal that the business may not be reliable to recommend.

2. Review volume, recency, and response rate

Gemini is pulling heavily from reviews — not just the star rating, but the content of reviews. Specific details about services, location, staff, and outcomes appear to increase the likelihood of being surfaced in AI answers.

What's changed: the response rate matters more than it used to. Businesses that respond to reviews — good and bad — signal active management, which correlates with businesses Gemini is more likely to recommend. A business with 50 reviews and zero responses is a weaker signal than one with 30 reviews and replies to all of them.

3. Consistent NAP across the web

NAP stands for Name, Address, Phone number. When your business details are consistent across your website, GBP, local directories (Yelp, Yellow Pages, industry-specific directories), and citation sites, Gemini can be confident it's surfacing accurate information.

Inconsistencies — your address formatted differently across different sites, an old phone number on a directory — reduce AI confidence in your business as a recommendation. This is an old SEO principle that matters more in the AI era, not less.

4. Content that directly answers questions

Traditional SEO rewarded content that targeted keywords. AI search rewards content that directly answers questions your customers actually ask. There's overlap, but the emphasis is different.

A page titled "Auto Detailing Services in Calgary" that's primarily a list of services and a contact form is less useful to Gemini than a page that answers "how long does a full detail take," "what's included in a paint correction," or "do you come to my location."

Gemini is looking for pages it can confidently cite as authoritative answers. That requires content with depth, specificity, and direct responses to real questions.

5. Schema markup and structured data

Schema markup tells search engines exactly what your content means — not just what it says. LocalBusiness schema, Service schema, FAQ schema, and Review schema all give Gemini more structured information to work with when building AI answers.

This is one of the most underutilized signals in local SEO. Most small business websites have no schema at all. Adding properly implemented structured data is a relatively low-effort way to make your business more legible to AI systems.

What doesn't change

Before you abandon your current SEO strategy, it's worth being clear about what hasn't changed:

  • Page speed and Core Web Vitals still matter. AI systems aren't going to recommend slow, unusable websites.
  • Domain authority and backlinks still matter. A business with authoritative links from local news, industry publications, or chambers of commerce is still more trustworthy to AI than one with no external validation.
  • Local citation consistency still matters. This is actually more important in the AI era.
  • Mobile optimization still matters. The majority of local searches happen on mobile.

The businesses that get hurt by AI search are those that were gaming traditional SEO — keyword-stuffed pages, thin content, purchased links. AI systems are better at detecting low-quality signals and ignoring them.

Practical steps to take now

Start with your GBP. This is the highest-leverage action for most local businesses. Update all information, add recent photos (at minimum monthly), respond to every review, and use the Posts feature to stay active.

Audit your NAP consistency. Search your business name and check how your address and phone number appear across 10–15 major directories. Fix inconsistencies — it's tedious but it matters.

Add FAQ content to your key service pages. Identify the 5–10 questions customers actually ask before booking, and answer them directly on your website. Add FAQ schema to these pages.

Build a review generation process. Ask every satisfied customer for a review — at the right moment (immediately after service, when they're still in the positive state). Make it frictionless: a direct link to your GBP review page, ideally sent via text message.

Get cited in local publications. One mention in a local newspaper, business association, or industry publication is worth more than dozens of generic directory citations. Sponsor a local event. Get interviewed by a local business podcast. Participate in a chamber of commerce list.

The honest take

AI search doesn't make local SEO obsolete. It raises the bar. The businesses that benefit from AI-generated answers are the ones that have done the fundamentals exceptionally well: complete and active GBP, genuine reviews, consistent citations, useful website content, and real community presence.

The businesses that get hurt are those that were surviving on technical SEO tricks without genuine quality signals underneath.

If you've been doing local SEO right — building real authority, earning real reviews, producing genuinely useful content — the AI shift is mostly good news for you. If you've been cutting corners, it's a signal to change course.
