AI search optimisation vs. traditional SEO: what's actually different

Most of the advice out there conflates the two. Here's a clear breakdown of what genuinely changes when AI generates search answers — and what stays exactly the same.

R
Rohan
Vault Marketing

The SEO industry has a tendency to rebrand existing work as "the new thing" whenever something changes in search. AI search is genuinely different in some important ways — but a lot of what's being sold as "AI SEO" or "GEO" (Generative Engine Optimisation) is just good content strategy with a new label.

This article is an attempt to be precise about what actually changes with AI-generated search answers and what doesn't. It's aimed at business owners and marketing managers who are trying to figure out whether they need to completely rethink their SEO or just adjust the emphasis.

The short answer: adjust the emphasis. The fundamentals don't change. But some things that were previously optional are now more important, and some things that used to drive rankings matter less.

What we mean by "AI search"

There are several different things people mean when they say AI search:

  • Google AI Overviews (formerly Search Generative Experience) — AI-generated summaries that appear at the top of some Google search results
  • ChatGPT / Perplexity / Claude — AI assistants that answer questions directly, sometimes with citations
  • Gemini — Google's AI assistant, increasingly integrated into search
  • Bing Copilot — Microsoft's AI-powered search results

These are meaningfully different products. Google AI Overviews appear within traditional Google search and pull from Google's index. ChatGPT and Perplexity are standalone products that use web browsing or separate indexes. What works for one doesn't perfectly map to the others.

For most businesses, Google AI Overviews matter most because that's where the majority of search traffic still lives. We'll focus there, with notes on what differs for other AI platforms.

What genuinely changes

The answer format shifts from links to synthesis

Traditional SEO was optimising to be the link someone clicks. AI search is optimising to be the source AI cites — or ideally, the business it recommends without the user needing to click anything.

This is a meaningful shift for informational queries ("what's the best SEO strategy for a small business") but has less impact on transactional and local queries ("best plumber near me"), where the Map Pack and direct listings still dominate.

Entity authority matters more than keyword density

Traditional SEO rewarded pages that mentioned keywords frequently and in the right places. AI systems reward entities — businesses, people, places, concepts — that have clear, consistent, authoritative signals across the web.

An entity is more than a keyword. "Vault Marketing" as an entity means: a business with a website, a Google Business Profile, consistent NAP across directories, mentions in publications, reviews, founder information, and a clear topical focus. When that entity profile is strong and consistent, AI systems are more likely to surface it.

Answer-format content performs better

Content written as a clear, direct answer to a specific question performs better in AI-generated responses than content written to target broad keyword terms. The distinction is subtle but important.

Keyword-targeted content: "Local SEO Calgary — Complete Guide to Ranking Your Business"
Answer-format content: "How long does local SEO take to work in Calgary?" followed by a direct, specific answer

FAQ sections, structured how-to content, and specific question-answer pairs are more likely to be cited by AI than long-form content that covers everything broadly.

Structured data is more important

Schema markup — the structured data that tells search engines explicitly what your content means — has always mattered for SEO. It matters more in the AI era because it makes your content machine-readable in a way that AI systems can use directly.

LocalBusiness schema, Service schema, FAQ schema, Article schema, and Review schema all provide structured information that AI can incorporate into generated answers with higher confidence than inferring the same information from unstructured text.

What stays exactly the same

Signal Traditional SEO AI Search
Page speed & Core Web Vitals Ranking factor Still required — slow pages won't be cited
Backlinks from authoritative sources Major ranking factor Still important — indicates trustworthiness
Content quality and depth Ranking factor More important — AI rewards expertise
Mobile optimisation Ranking factor Unchanged
NAP consistency (local) Local ranking factor More important — AI needs consistent entity data
Google Business Profile Local ranking factor More important — primary AI data source for local

The businesses that get hurt by AI search are those whose SEO was built on thin signals: keyword-stuffed content, purchased links, thin local citations with no supporting signals. AI systems are better at detecting and ignoring low-quality signals than traditional algorithms.

What matters less now

Exact keyword matching

AI language models understand semantic meaning. Ranking for "best plumber Calgary" requires less exact keyword repetition than it used to. What matters is clearly establishing that your business provides plumbing services, is located in Calgary, and is trustworthy and authoritative.

This doesn't mean keywords are irrelevant. It means writing naturally for humans while ensuring the key entities (service, location, business name) are clearly present — rather than mechanically repeating exact phrases.

Thin informational pages

A page that's 300 words and targets a keyword with no real depth, no specific answers, no useful information for the reader — this was barely useful in traditional SEO and is essentially invisible to AI systems. The bar for "worth indexing" has risen.

What's genuinely new for AI citation optimisation

If you want to be cited in AI-generated answers (as opposed to just ranked in traditional results), there are a few tactics that specifically help:

  • Get mentioned by authoritative publications. ChatGPT, Perplexity, and other AI systems use training data and web browsing. Being mentioned in industry publications, local news, and authoritative directories increases the probability of being cited.
  • Build a clear "about" entity page. A clear, factual About page with your founding date, location, team, and service areas gives AI systems the factual basis to accurately describe your business in generated answers.
  • Create content that directly answers the questions AI is being asked. Use tools like AnswerThePublic, Reddit, or Google's "People Also Ask" to find the specific questions in your industry and write direct, authoritative answers to them.
  • Maintain consistent presence on data aggregators. Factual claims about businesses — hours, services, location — that appear consistently across multiple sources are more likely to be cited confidently by AI systems.

The practical implication

If you're doing SEO well already, AI search is mostly good news. If you've been cutting corners, it's a signal to change direction — not a new set of tricks to apply on top of the same weak foundation.

The businesses that are positioned to benefit from AI search are those with: strong entity profiles (consistent GBP, NAP, and citations), genuine authority signals (real backlinks, real reviews, real mentions), answer-format content for the questions customers actually ask, and proper structured data implementation.

None of that is new. What's new is the relative weight placed on each signal and the specific format that performs best in AI-generated answers. The direction of travel is clear: be a genuinely authoritative source on your topic, for your location, for your customers. That hasn't changed.
