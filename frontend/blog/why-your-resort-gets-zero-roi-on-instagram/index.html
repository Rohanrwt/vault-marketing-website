Why your resort gets zero ROI from Instagram (and what to do instead)

Follower counts are not a business metric. Here's why Instagram is the wrong primary channel for most hospitality brands — and where the actual revenue in hotel and resort marketing comes from.

R
Rohan
Vault Marketing

Every resort and boutique hotel we've talked to in the last two years has an Instagram account. Most of them have a social media manager or an agency posting three times a week. Most of them have no idea whether any of it translates to bookings.

When we ask "how do you track the ROI on your social media spend?" the answer is almost always some version of: follower growth, engagement rate, or "brand awareness." None of those are revenue metrics.

This isn't an anti-Instagram argument. Instagram can play a useful role in hospitality marketing. The problem is that most hospitality brands treat it as their primary marketing channel and measure success by metrics that don't connect to occupancy, revenue per available room, or direct booking rate.

Here's why that happens and what to do differently.

The fundamental problem: intent

Instagram is a discovery platform. When someone is scrolling Instagram, they are not actively looking to book a hotel room. They might see a beautiful photo of your resort and feel inspired. That's it. They've been exposed to your brand. The vast majority of them will not go directly from that scroll to booking a room.

This is what marketers call an intent gap. The person's intent at the moment of seeing your content (passive browsing) does not match the intent required to take the action you want (active booking decision).

Compare this to Google Search. When someone types "resort in Rishikesh with river view" into Google, they are actively in booking mode. They have the intent. The only question is whether you appear when they search — and whether your direct booking channel is good enough to capture them before they go to an OTA.

Instagram can build the brand impression that eventually leads someone to Google you. But it almost never closes the booking directly. You're running a top-of-funnel awareness channel and measuring it against bottom-of-funnel conversion metrics, which is why the numbers never make sense.

Where hospitality brands actually should invest

1. Google Search — both SEO and paid

High-intent hospitality searches — "best resort in [location]," "[location] hotel with [specific amenity]," "where to stay in [destination] for [occasion]" — are among the highest-value search queries in any category. Someone who searches this is in the consideration phase and will book within days, not months.

If you're ranking on page one for your core destination keywords, you're capturing guests who were actively looking for you (or someone like you) at exactly the right moment. If you're not ranking, you're handing those guests to your competitors and to OTAs — then paying OTA commissions to get them back.

Paid Google Search for hospitality is expensive but the intent quality is so high that cost-per-booking is often lower than any other channel. Especially when combined with Google Hotel Ads, which surfaces your direct booking price alongside OTA prices directly in the search results.

2. Direct booking SEO and conversion rate

The single highest-leverage action for most hospitality brands is reducing OTA dependency by improving direct booking. OTA commissions run 15–25%. A guest who books directly is worth significantly more than the same guest booking through Booking.com or Expedia, at the same room rate.

The levers for direct booking are: SEO (rank so guests find you directly instead of through an OTA), website conversion (your direct booking experience must be genuinely better than the OTA experience — easier, with better pricing or offers), and price parity maintenance (if your OTA price and direct price are the same, give guests a reason to book direct — early check-in, free upgrade, breakfast, anything).

We've seen this work directly. A resort in Rishikesh we worked with went from 80% OTA-dependent to a meaningfully higher direct booking share within 18 months — generating over ₹10Cr in revenue through the direct channel. The mechanism: SEO to capture organic intent, conversion-optimized website to close the booking, and a direct booking incentive to compete with OTA rates.

3. Google Hotel Ads

Google Hotel Ads show your direct booking price in comparison to OTA prices directly in Google Search results. When a guest is comparing where to book, seeing your direct price alongside OTA prices — often with no commission incentive making them higher — removes one of the main reasons people default to OTAs (trust and price comparison).

This is underutilized by independent hotels and resorts. The setup requires a price feed integration and some configuration, but the ROI is typically very strong because you're intercepting guests at exactly the moment they're deciding where to book.

4. Retargeting — using Instagram correctly

Here's where Instagram does play a legitimate role: retargeting. A guest who has already visited your website, browsed room categories, maybe started a booking but didn't complete it — that person has demonstrated intent. They know who you are. They were close to booking.

Showing that person a targeted Instagram or Facebook ad — with a specific offer, or simply a reminder of what they were looking at — is a high-value use of social advertising. The intent gap is closed because you know this person was already in booking mode.

The correct role for Instagram in hospitality: brand awareness at the top of funnel, retargeting at the bottom. Not as the primary channel. Not measured by follower growth. Measured by assisted bookings and retargeting conversion rate.

5. Email to past guests

Your best prospect for a future booking is someone who has already stayed at your property and had a positive experience. They know you. They trust you. The cost of acquiring them again is essentially zero.

Most hospitality businesses underinvest in past-guest email. A simple sequence — seasonal offers, return guest discounts, new amenity announcements — consistently outperforms any social media channel on a per-contact basis. And unlike social media, you own the list. Instagram can cut your organic reach to zero overnight. Your email list is yours.

How to measure hospitality marketing properly

The metrics that matter:

  • Direct booking rate — what percentage of bookings come through your direct channel vs. OTAs
  • Cost per direct booking — total marketing spend divided by direct bookings (compare this to OTA commission cost per booking)
  • Revenue per available room (RevPAR) — the standard hospitality metric; make sure your marketing activity is moving this
  • Organic search visibility — are you ranking for the terms your ideal guests search? Track position for your core destination queries
  • Channel mix — what percentage of revenue comes from each channel? Are you reducing OTA dependency over time?

The metrics that don't matter:

  • Instagram followers
  • Engagement rate on social posts
  • "Brand awareness" with no conversion tie-in
  • Website sessions without booking conversion rate
  • Impressions on any channel

The honest take

Instagram is a beautiful platform for hospitality content. Your resort probably photographs well. You probably have a social media person who cares about this and does good work. I'm not saying delete the account.

I'm saying: Instagram is not a revenue channel for most hospitality brands, and treating it like one is causing you to under-invest in channels that actually move bookings — specifically Google Search, direct booking conversion, and retargeting.

The hospitality brands that build sustainable, OTA-independent revenue streams are doing so through search visibility and direct booking investment. The ones that plateau at 70%+ OTA dependency are the ones that measure success by Instagram metrics and wonder why the unit economics never improve.

Measure what moves revenue. Invest where intent lives. Use Instagram for what it's actually good at.
